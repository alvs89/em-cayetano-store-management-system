CHAPTER 1
Introduction
In the current digital business environment, small and medium-sized enterprises increasingly depend on information systems to improve operational accuracy, accountability, and responsiveness. Hardware and construction supply stores are especially dependent on accurate records because sales, purchases, deliveries, inventory balances, and branch-level stock availability directly affect customer service and replenishment decisions. When these processes are handled manually or through disconnected spreadsheets, the business becomes vulnerable to delayed reporting, inconsistent data, duplicate encoding, weak access control, and difficulty tracing the origin of record discrepancies.

Software engineering provides the discipline necessary to address these operational concerns through planned analysis, iterative design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance. According to McNeil (2024), software engineering applies engineering principles to the full software life cycle so that systems are not only functional but also reliable, secure, usable, and adaptable. This study applies that principle by developing a web-based system that does not merely digitize individual records, but organizes related business workflows into a centralized store management platform.

The proposed Web-Based Store Management System for E.M. Cayetano Trading is designed to support daily branch operations through secured access, role-based permissions, product and branch inventory management, sales recording, supplier purchase receiving, stock movement tracking, reporting, archiving, audit trails, and maintenance utilities. The system reflects the actual relationship between inventory and transactions: sales decrease available stock, supplier purchases increase stock, manual adjustments are recorded as stock movements, and reports consolidate these activities into useful summaries for management review.

The development approach for the project has shifted from the traditional V-Model to the Agile Model. This change is significant because the system's scope evolved through repeated review of business needs and working software. Agile development is more appropriate for this project because it supports incremental delivery, user feedback, reprioritization, and continuous validation of implemented features. Rather than treating requirements as fixed at the beginning of the project, the proponents refined the system through iterations that produced concrete modules such as sales history, purchase receiving, audit logging, backup and restore, and expanded role permissions.

As a web-based platform, the system allows authorized users to access business records through a browser, reducing dependency on isolated files and manual consolidation. It provides centralized storage through PostgreSQL, server-side processing through Express.js, and a responsive user interface through React. These technologies enable real-time access to branch data, structured validation of transactions, secure authentication, and consistent record retrieval for reports and administrative review. Through this integrated approach, the system supports E.M. Cayetano Trading in improving operational accuracy, business visibility, and readiness for future expansion.

Background of the Study
E.M. Cayetano Trading is a hardware and construction supply business established on May 25, 2012, by Ms. Edna M. Cayetano, who continues to serve as the owner and proprietor. Its main branch is located in Manggahan, Rodriguez, Rizal, while another branch operates in San Rafael, Rodriguez, Rizal. The business offers products commonly used in construction and home improvement, including cement, plywood, roofing materials, PVC pipes and fittings, steel bars, electrical supplies, paints, tools, and other hardware items.

The existing operational process relies on manual records and Microsoft Excel for inventory and daily transaction monitoring. At the end of daily operations, sales and purchases are encoded, stock balances are computed, and physical counts are compared against recorded quantities. The store uses the practical inventory formula: Beginning Inventory - Outgoing Sales + New Deliveries = Ending Inventory. Although this formula is straightforward, the manual process surrounding it is susceptible to human error, delayed updating, and inconsistent branch records.

The interview with the business owner established that inventory accuracy is the primary operational concern. However, the implemented system recognizes that inventory accuracy depends on the quality of related workflows. Sales must be recorded with sufficient detail to deduct tracked items, distinguish non-inventory items, handle discounts, payment methods, VAT breakdowns, refunds, and cancellations, and preserve official sales invoice numbers. Supplier deliveries and purchases must likewise be encoded as receiving records so that new stock is added to the appropriate branch inventory and linked to supplier, document, payment, quantity, and cost information.

The proposed system therefore expands beyond a narrow inventory application into a store management system that connects inventory, sales, purchases, reporting, auditing, and maintenance. The Sales Module records point-of-sale transactions, official numeric sales invoice references, customer details, payment confirmation, receipt generation, sales history, refunds, and administrative cancellations. The Purchases Module records supplier receiving entries, delivery documents, payment terms, item-level costs, and stock increases. The Reports Module provides inventory snapshots and activity-based reports, including summary, detailed inventory, low-stock alerts, supplier reorder reports, untracked sales items, category analysis, purchase reports, stock movement history, actual earnings, and sales-based stock movement reports based on user role.

The system is implemented using HTML5, CSS3, and React v19 for the front end; JavaScript (ES2024) and Express.js version 5.1.0 for the back end; and PostgreSQL version 18 for persistent data storage. It uses bcrypt password hashing, JSON Web Token authentication, email-based one-time password verification, role-based access control, database-level constraints, and audit logs to strengthen security and accountability. These features address not only record accuracy but also traceability, operational control, and resilience.

By adopting a web-based and Agile-developed solution, E.M. Cayetano Trading can reduce delays in branch monitoring, minimize discrepancies caused by manual encoding, and improve the visibility of sales, purchasing, and stock movement data. The system also establishes a maintainable foundation for future improvements because Agile development encourages iterative enhancement based on user feedback and operational evidence.

Statement of the Problem
E.M. Cayetano Trading currently relies on manual and spreadsheet-based processes that are insufficient for accurate, secure, and timely management of inventory, sales, purchases, and branch-level operational records. Specifically, the study addresses the following problems:

1. Weak security and accountability in record handling, resulting in limited control over who can view, create, modify, cancel, restore, or maintain business records.
2. Lack of centralized and real-time visibility over inventory balances, stock movements, sales transactions, and supplier purchase entries across the Manggahan and San Rafael branches.
3. Inefficient sales and purchase recording, where daily outgoing sales and incoming deliveries require manual encoding and reconciliation before inventory balances can be verified.
4. Limited reporting capability, making it difficult for the admin to review low-stock items, supplier reorder needs, sales-based stock movement, purchase history, actual earnings, and category performance in a consistent format.
5. Inefficient user and system administration, including account approval, role assignment, branch assignment, password recovery, audit review, backup, restore, log cleanup, database optimization, and data integrity checking.

Objectives of the Study
The general objective of this study is to develop a Web-Based Store Management System for E.M. Cayetano Trading that improves accuracy, efficiency, security, and accountability in branch operations.

Specifically, the study aims to:

1. Implement a secure authentication and authorization module using role-based access control, bcrypt password hashing, JSON Web Tokens, and email-based one-time password verification.
2. Provide centralized inventory management for branch stock records, including product information, categories, stock levels, reorder thresholds, supplier details, cost values, selling prices, stock movements, archiving, and restoration.
3. Develop a Sales Module that supports point-of-sale recording, official sales invoice numbering, customer details, discounts, VAT computation, payment methods, payment confirmation, receipt generation, sales history, refunds, and administrative cancellation.
4. Develop a Purchases Module that records supplier deliveries, document types, document numbers, payment terms, item-level costs, total quantities, and automatic stock increases for the appropriate branch.
5. Provide reporting tools that generate inventory, sales, purchase, supplier reorder, low-stock, stock movement, category, untracked sales, and earnings information according to user role and selected reporting period.
6. Integrate administrative features for user management, audit trail review, database backup, restore, log cleanup, database optimization, and read-only data integrity checking.
7. Apply an Agile development methodology that supports iterative requirements refinement, incremental implementation, feedback-based improvement, and continuous validation of the system against business needs.

Significance of the Study
The study is significant to E.M. Cayetano Trading because it provides a centralized platform for monitoring core store operations. The admin gains improved visibility over branch inventory, sales, purchases, reports, users, and system health. This supports better decision-making, reduces dependence on manual reconciliation, and helps prevent losses caused by overstocking, understocking, inaccurate sales records, or delayed purchase updates.

Employees benefit from clearer and more structured workflows. Sales Encoders can record sales, generate receipts, and review sales history within their assigned operational context. Inventory staff can monitor stock, encode supplier receiving entries, access inventory reports, and respond to low-stock conditions. The system reduces repetitive manual work and provides immediate feedback when entries are incomplete, invalid, or outside the user's role permissions.

The business also benefits from stronger accountability. Audit trails, stock movement history, backup logs, and system logs make it easier to review important events and administrative actions. The separation of user roles between Admin, Sales Encoder, and Inventory Staff ensures that sensitive functions such as account management, database restoration, cancellation of sales, and audit review remain under administrative control.

For the proponents, the study provides practical experience in software engineering, database design, web application development, security, system testing, and Agile project management. The iterative development process strengthens the proponents' ability to analyze changing requirements, deliver working increments, evaluate feedback, and revise the system without losing coherence in the overall design.

For future researchers, the study may serve as a reference for developing web-based store management systems for small and medium-sized enterprises. It demonstrates how inventory management can be integrated with sales, purchases, reports, auditability, and administrative maintenance in a coherent system architecture.

Scope
The proposed system covers the development of a web-based store management platform for E.M. Cayetano Trading. It is designed for authorized business users and supports the Manggahan and San Rafael branches. The system includes modules for security, dashboard monitoring, inventory, search, archive, reports, sales, purchases, alerts, maintenance, user management, audit trail, and help.

The Security Module manages login, two-factor verification, password recovery, password change, token-based sessions, password policy validation, and role-based access. User registration is administratively controlled through account approval, rejection, role assignment, branch assignment, activation, and deactivation. The supported roles are Admin, Sales Encoder, and Inventory Staff.

The Inventory Module manages the product catalog and branch-specific stock records. It stores product names, categories, suppliers, prices, costs, stock levels, minimum stock thresholds, lead times, safety stock, average daily sales values, and status values such as In Stock, Low Stock, and Out of Stock. It also records stock movements, including stock in, stock out, initial stock, and adjustment actions.

The Sales Module supports customer-facing sales records and sales history. It records official six-digit sales invoice numbers, system sales references, customer type, customer name, tax identification number, address, items sold, tracked and non-inventory items, discounts, delivery charges, VAT, payment method, payment confirmation, total amounts, receipts, refunds, backdated entries with reasons, and admin-controlled cancellations.

The Purchases Module records supplier receiving transactions. It stores supplier name, document type, document number, payment terms, remarks, item-level quantities, unit costs, subtotals, previous and new quantities, and the user who encoded the transaction. Purchase entries automatically add stock to the applicable branch inventory.

The Reports Module provides both inventory snapshot reports and activity-based reports. Supported report types include Summary, Detailed Inventory, Low Stock Alert, Supplier Reorder Report, Untracked Sales Items, Category Analysis, Purchases, Stock Movement History, Actual Earnings, and Sales-Based Stock Movement. Access to specific report types depends on the user's role.

The Archive Module stores inactive inventory records so that discontinued or removed items can be reviewed and restored by an administrator when appropriate. The Alerts Module notifies users about low-stock and out-of-stock items, pending user accounts, backup status, and recent maintenance events. The Search Module supports efficient product lookup and filtering. The Help Module provides in-system assistance for users.

The Maintenance Module is limited to authorized administrators and supports database backup, database restore, eligible system log cleanup, database optimization, and read-only data integrity checking. The Audit Trail Module records significant user actions for accountability and administrative review.

The system uses React v19, HTML5, and CSS3 for the client interface; JavaScript (ES2024) and Express.js version 5.1.0 for server-side processing; and PostgreSQL version 18 for database management. Algorithms such as linear search, binary search, merge sort, and bcrypt hashing are applied to support search, sorting, and security requirements. The system is intended for browser-based use on supported devices and operating systems.

Delimitations
The system is intended for internal use by E.M. Cayetano Trading's admin and authorized employees. It does not provide a public customer ordering website, online marketplace, customer relationship management module, payroll module, accounting ledger, or direct supplier communication portal. Although the Purchases Module records supplier receiving entries, it does not send orders to suppliers or automate supplier negotiation.

The system depends on internet connectivity and a functioning server and database environment. It is limited to supported browsers and operating systems identified in the scope and does not guarantee complete compatibility with legacy browsers, unsupported devices, or offline operation.

The initial deliverable does not include full migration of historical spreadsheet or paper records into the database. Existing records may be encoded manually or prepared separately before operational use. Post-deployment enhancements, long-term hosting administration, and continuous business process consulting are outside the initial project deliverables.

The system improves security through authentication, authorization, password hashing, role control, validation, audit logs, and backup procedures; however, it cannot guarantee absolute protection from all external threats, hardware failures, hosting failures, credential misuse, or unforeseen data loss.

System Design
System design is the process of transforming user requirements into a
structured blueprint that defines how a system will operate and interact with its
components. According to Lal (2024), system design is both an innovative and
technical phase of development, where the proposed solution is planned and
organized in preparation for implementation. It focuses on the “how” of the
system such as how processes are structured and components interact to
achieve organizational goals. Tsui et al. (2023) emphasize that system design
bridges the gap between requirements and implementation by decomposing the
system into interconnected modules, ensuring that every function, process, and
interface works cohesively to meet both functional and non-functional
requirements.
To begin with, to visualize and communicate the design effectively, various
diagrams are used as graphical notations. These diagrams help analysts,
designers, and developers understand the system’s structure and behavior in a
more intuitive and organized manner. Each type of diagram serves a unique
purpose and represents a different perspective of the system.
Among these diagrams, the Flowchart is one of the most fundamental
tools used in system design. It graphically represents the logical flow of
processes using standardized symbols such as ovals, rectangles, and diamonds
to denote start or end points, actions, and decisions. According to Kung (2023),
flowcharts function as directed graphs that organize processing nodes, decision
13
nodes, and connecting paths to effectively model the flow of control within a
process.
Meanwhile, a Data Flow Diagram (DFD) emphasizes how data moves
between processes and entities rather than control flow. According to Borg
(2024), DFDs serve as significant modeling techniques for analyzing and
constructing information processes by illustrating the movement of information
based on inputs and outputs within a system. DFDs are important because they
model information systems clearly, allowing designers to trace data from entry to
exit points.
In addition, the Use Case Diagram, as Tsui et al. (2023) describe it as a
representation of the interactions between users (actors) and the system. It helps
identify system functionalities from the users' perspective and ensures that all
interactions and requirements are well understood during system design.
Furthermore, modern system design continues to integrate various
modeling techniques while retaining classical methods such as the Hierarchical
Input-Process-Output (HIPO) diagram. The HIPO model adopts a top-down
approach that visualizes a system’s overall structure through hierarchy charts
and Input-Process-Output (IPO) diagrams. This method effectively documents
system functions, clarifies relationships among modules, and supports clear
communication between designers and stakeholders.
Moreover, Input-Process-Output (IPO) diagrams, as described in the
INCOSE Systems Engineering Handbook (2023), represent how inputs are
14
transformed into outputs through defined system processes. Each IPO diagram
outlines what data enters a process, how it is handled, and what results it
produces. This structured visualization helps system engineers and developers
identify the essential components and expected outcomes of each module.
Lastly, the Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) focuses on the structure of
data in the system. Based on Borg (2024), an ERD "visually depicts an
organization's entities, the entities' relationships to each other, and the business
rules associated with the relationships" (p. 52). It provides a foundation for
database design by defining entities, attributes, and their interconnections.
Narrative Description of the Existing System
The existing system of E.M. Cayetano Trading operates entirely through
manual, paper-based, and spreadsheet-driven processes for managing daily
operations. The workflow mainly involves employees recording daily sales,
deliveries, and inventory updates using Microsoft Excel files. Each branch,
namely Manggahan and San Rafael, maintains its own records independently,
meaning that the admin does not consolidate or centralize their data. This
branch-by-branch setup makes it difficult to gain a unified view of stock
availability and overall business performance. The system lacks automation,
real-time tracking, and centralized storage, which often causes inconsistencies
between recorded quantities and actual stock levels.
In the current setup, each employee manually logs item transactions, such
as stock-in (deliveries) and stock-out (sales), into spreadsheets. Afterward,
15
physical stock counts are conducted at the end of the day or week to cross-check
these records. Any discrepancies require manual reconciliation, often involving
recalculating the ending inventory and verifying purchase or delivery documents.
The process is repetitive, slow, and highly prone to human error—especially
when multiple employees update the same file simultaneously.
Furthermore, since both branches operate separately, tracking inventory
across locations is difficult. The Admin must manually collect and merge files
from both locations to determine overall stock availability. This lack of
synchronization often results in overstocking of slow-moving products or
shortages of fast-moving items. Additionally, because there are no automated
low-stock alerts, restocking only happens after employees notice that supplies
are running low, which can disrupt operations or delay customer orders.
Reporting also presents significant challenges. Employees must manually
compile stock and sales data into Excel summary sheets and send them to the
admin for review. Preparing daily, weekly, or monthly inventory summaries
requires gathering multiple files, verifying formulas, and recalculating totals,
increasing the risk of misreporting. The absence of standardized reporting tools
makes it difficult for the admin to quickly assess inventory performance or detect
trends.
Moreover, the system lacks any built-in user management or data security
features. All employees share access to the same files, so accidental deletions,
unauthorized edits, or untracked changes can easily occur without accountability.
16
Overall, the existing manual system is inefficient and error-prone, limiting
the company’s ability to maintain accurate inventory records, generate timely
reports, and ensure secure data handling across branches. These limitations
highlight the need for a web-based, centralized store management system that can support semi-automated inventory monitoring, sales recording, purchase receiving, reporting, and user access control, helping streamline operations and support better business decisions for both branches.
Narrative Description of the Proposed System
The proposed system is a web-based store management platform developed specifically for E.M. Cayetano Trading to replace its manual, paper-based, and spreadsheet-driven processes. It aims to improve the company's inventory monitoring, sales and purchase recording, reporting accuracy, and data security across its multiple branches. By centralizing all records in a single online database, the
system enables the admin and authorized employees to access real-time,
consistent information anytime and anywhere through a secure internet
connection.
The system provides role-based access control to ensure organized and
secure user interaction. The admin holds the highest level of access, enabling
them to manage products, review reports, perform maintenance tasks such as
data backup and restoration, and oversee user accounts. Meanwhile, employees are granted limited access according to role, such as recording sales, receiving supplier purchases, updating stock movements, searching items, and viewing product details. This role-based control ensures that sensitive functions—like deactivating
17
accounts, approving new users, or editing high-level records remain exclusive to
admin.
The proposed system is composed of several interrelated modules that
enhance usability and reliability. The Security Module ensures that only
authorized users can log in through role-based authentication, secure password
handling, and account verification. The Registration and Verification Module
supports new account creation, email verification, and password reset processes,
ensuring accessibility and preventing duplicate user entries.
For system upkeep, the Maintenance Module allows an admin to perform data backups, restore records when needed, clear eligible system logs, optimize database tables, check data integrity, and manage user profiles. A Help
Module is also integrated to assist users by providing FAQs, system guidelines,
and contact support, minimizing confusion and errors during use.
Central to the system is the Inventory Module, where all stock records are stored and updated. Sales transactions deduct tracked items, supplier purchase entries add received items, and manual adjustments are recorded as stock movements while the system automatically calculates balances. Supporting this, the Search
Module enables fast and accurate item lookups.
Supporting business oversight, the Reports Module provides
comprehensive reporting features to support business analysis and
decision-making. It generates standardized daily, weekly, and monthly reports
based on the company’s formula. In addition to time-based summaries, the
system also features content-based report views and types, such as Summary, Detailed Inventory, Low Stock Alert, Supplier Reorder, Untracked Sales Items, Category Analysis, Purchases, Stock Movement History, Actual Earnings, and Sales-Based Stock Movement, depending on user role. These reports allow the admin and authorized employees to analyze inventory trends, transaction history, profitability, and operational performance from different perspectives. The module also includes export options for documentation and review.
To further support operational accuracy, the Alerts and Notifications
Module provides real-time low-stock alerts and system-related notifications,
ensuring that the admins and employees can make timely restocking decisions
and respond promptly to inventory changes.
Complementing this, the Archive Module stores inactive or outdated
inventory items in a secure storage area within the system. This allows an admin
to manage records efficiently—viewing, filtering, or restoring archived items when
necessary while maintaining a clean and organized active inventory list.
From a technical standpoint, the system will be developed using HTML5
and CSS3 for structure and design, ensuring a clean and responsive interface.
The front end will be powered by React v19, providing dynamic and interactive
user experiences. On the server side, the system will utilize JavaScript (ES2024)
with Express.js version 5.1.0 to handle backend logic and enable seamless
communication between the client and server. For data storage, PostgreSQL
(v18) will serve as the main database, offering reliable, scalable, and structured
record management for inventory, sales, purchase, audit, and maintenance data across branches. The system will remain
accessible across devices and browsers, supporting both desktop and mobile
platforms through common browsers like Google Chrome and Mozilla Firefox.
19
Overall, the system strengthens operational efficiency by semi-automating stock management, structuring sales and purchase records, securing sensitive data, and improving operational visibility across branches. With clear role assignments and centralized data access, E.M.
Cayetano Trading can minimize errors, generate timely reports, and make
data-driven decisions, establishing a stronger foundation for business growth and
sustainability.

CHAPTER 3
Methodology
This chapter discusses the methodology used in developing the Web-Based Store Management System for E.M. Cayetano Trading. The project adopts the Agile Model as its software development methodology. The shift from the traditional V-Model to Agile is appropriate because the system evolved from a primarily inventory-focused concept into a broader store management platform that integrates inventory, sales, purchases, reports, audit trails, user management, and maintenance operations. Agile supports this type of development because it allows requirements to be refined through iterations, encourages regular review of working software, and enables the proponents to respond to newly clarified business needs without disrupting the entire project structure.

The methodology covers the software process model, system architecture, data-gathering instruments, feasibility study, cost-benefit analysis, materials, testing and operating procedure, software evaluation, sampling tools, work plan, algorithm implementation, and algorithm architecture. These sections establish how the system was planned, developed, evaluated, and aligned with E.M. Cayetano Trading's operational requirements.

Software Process Model
Figure 86 Agile Model Diagram
The Agile Model is an iterative and incremental software development approach in which a system is built through repeated cycles of planning, design, implementation, testing, review, and refinement. Unlike strictly sequential models, Agile recognizes that software requirements may become clearer as users examine working features. For this project, Agile was used to manage changing requirements, validate implemented modules early, and improve the system based on observed business workflows.

The Agile process followed by the proponents consisted of the following recurring activities:

1. Requirement discovery and prioritization. The proponents gathered initial requirements through interview, observation, and research. Requirements were then organized according to business priority, with inventory accuracy, branch monitoring, user security, sales recording, and purchase receiving treated as high-value features.
2. Iteration planning. Each development cycle focused on a defined set of modules or improvements, such as authentication, inventory records, sales history, purchase receiving, reports, audit logging, or maintenance utilities. This allowed the proponents to deliver visible progress while maintaining a coherent system direction.
3. Design and prototyping. User interface layouts, database relationships, and module flows were drafted before implementation. Design decisions were adjusted when the actual workflow required more precise controls, such as official sales invoice numbering, payment confirmation, backdated transaction reasons, or role-specific report access.
4. Implementation. The proponents implemented each increment using React, Express.js, and PostgreSQL. Client-side validation, server-side validation, database constraints, and role checks were incorporated to ensure that business rules were consistently enforced.
5. Testing and review. Implemented features were tested at unit, module, integration, and system levels. Review activities focused on correctness, usability, security, data consistency, and alignment with store operations.
6. Refinement and documentation. Feedback and defects identified during review were addressed in succeeding iterations. Documentation was updated to reflect the actual system scope, including the transition from an inventory-only description to a store management system with sales, purchases, reporting, auditability, and maintenance.

The Agile Model therefore supports the system's need for continuous improvement. It permits the proponents to respond to operational discoveries while still maintaining formal documentation, testing discipline, and traceability of requirements.

System Architecture
Figure 87 System Architecture
The system architecture for the Web-Based Store Management System follows a three-tier structure composed of the Presentation Tier, Logic Tier, and Data Tier. This architecture separates the user interface, business processing, and database storage so that each layer can be developed, tested, maintained, and improved independently.

The Presentation Tier is implemented using React v19, HTML5, and CSS3. It provides responsive interfaces for dashboard monitoring, inventory management, archive review, report generation, sales recording, purchase receiving, alerts, maintenance, user management, audit trail review, search, and help. The interface adapts available screens and controls according to user role. Admin users have access to system-wide administrative functions, Sales Encoders primarily handle sales recording, and Inventory Staff manage stock-related activities such as inventory review, purchase receiving, archive viewing, and inventory reports.

The Logic Tier is implemented using JavaScript (ES2024) and Express.js version 5.1.0. It handles authentication, two-factor verification, password recovery, role-based authorization, sales and purchase processing, inventory updates, stock movement recording, report data retrieval, audit logging, backup and restore requests, log cleanup, database optimization, and data integrity checking. This tier validates user inputs and enforces business rules before records are stored or modified.

The Data Tier uses PostgreSQL version 18. It stores users, products, branch inventory, stock movements, sales transactions, sales items, purchase transactions, purchase items, archived inventory, audit logs, backup logs, branch settings, system logs, and invoice number sequences. The schema includes constraints and relationships that preserve record consistency, such as unique official sales invoice numbers per branch, defined user roles, allowed transaction statuses, and foreign-key relationships between transaction headers and item lines.

Data flow begins when a user performs an action in the web interface. The client validates basic input and sends the request to the server. The server authenticates the user, verifies the user's role, applies the relevant business rules, and reads from or writes to the database. The server then returns structured results to the client, where updated records, alerts, reports, or confirmation messages are displayed. This layered exchange supports secure, accurate, and responsive system behavior.

Algorithm Implementation
The algorithm implementation of the Web-Based Store Management System supports performance, organization, and security. The system applies search, sorting, and hashing algorithms where they are appropriate to the implemented workflows.

Linear search is used for flexible filtering and lookup operations, especially when the dataset is unsorted or when the user performs keyword-based matching across product names, categories, suppliers, stock status, or report conditions. It is simple, reliable, and appropriate for moderate datasets where conditions may vary.

Binary search is used when records are sorted and the system needs faster lookup of specific values. Because binary search repeatedly divides the search area in half, it improves lookup efficiency when the dataset is already ordered.

Merge sort is used to organize records into stable and predictable order for display and reporting. It is appropriate for lists that may contain duplicate values, such as multiple products within the same category or stock status. Consistent ordering improves readability and supports efficient search and reporting.

Bcrypt hashing is used to protect user passwords. The system does not store plain-text passwords. Instead, passwords are transformed into secure hashes with salts. During login, the submitted password is hashed and compared with the stored hash. This protects credentials even if unauthorized access to the database occurs.

System Architecture of Algorithm
Figure 89 Linear Search Algorithm Architecture
The linear search algorithm checks records sequentially until a matching item is found or until all records have been evaluated. In this system, it supports general product search, report filtering, status filtering, category filtering, and other flexible lookup tasks where the list may not be sorted.

Figure 90 Binary Search Algorithm Architecture
The binary search algorithm is applied to sorted records. It compares the target value with the middle item, removes half of the search area from consideration, and repeats the process until the target is found or no matching record exists. This approach supports efficient lookup when the system has an ordered dataset.

Figure 91 Merge Sort Algorithm Architecture
The merge sort algorithm divides a list into smaller parts, sorts each part, and merges the sorted parts into a complete ordered list. In the system, it helps present product, inventory, purchase, sales, and report data in a consistent sequence.

Figure 92 Bcrypt Hashing Algorithm Architecture
The bcrypt hashing algorithm converts a user's password into a protected hash using a salt and computational cost factor. The stored hash is used for password verification, while the original password is never saved. This strengthens the system's security module and protects employee credentials.
