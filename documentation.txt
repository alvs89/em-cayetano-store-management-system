CHAPTER 1
Introduction
In today’s digital era, many businesses rely on technology to improve their
efficiency, accuracy, and competitiveness. For small and medium-sized
enterprises (SMEs), computerized systems have become essential for managing
daily operations, including inventories, employee records, and transactions.
Without such systems, businesses often face issues such as inaccurate records,
delays in reporting, and difficulties in coordinating across branches.
In this context, software engineering provides a structured and disciplined
approach to solving business challenges through technology. According to
McNeil (2024), software engineering applies engineering principles traditionally
used in the design and management of physical systems to the entire software
life cycle, including design, development, testing, deployment, and maintenance.
This ensures that the resulting software is not only functional but also efficient,
reliable, and adaptable to change. In simple terms, software engineering focuses
on building quality systems that can support long-term business growth and
continuous improvement.
McNeil (2024) also explains that computer software refers to the collection
of programs, procedures, and documentation that enable a computer system to
perform specific tasks. It is classified into three major types: system software,
which manages hardware and system resources; application software, which
helps users perform specific tasks such as word processing or inventory
management; and programming software, which provides the tools developers
1
use to create and maintain applications. Together, these types form the
foundation of every computerized system and make automation and integration
possible in modern business operations.
The concept of a system, an organized set of components working
together toward a goal lies at the center of software engineering and computer
software. In business, systems are used to automate processes, reduce errors,
and support decision-making. This is especially valuable for SMEs with multiple
branches, where manual methods such as spreadsheets often result in
inefficiency and inconsistent reporting.
A web-based system addresses these issues. Accessible through the
Internet, this type of system allows authorized users to log in and update records
in real time, regardless of their location. Web-based systems eliminate delays in
data consolidation, reduce inconsistencies, and provide scalability, since new
branches can be integrated easily without separate installations. They are also
cost-efficient, compatible with multiple devices and operating systems, and
capable of supporting continuous updates and improvements.
Building on these principles, this study proposes the development of a
Web-Based Store Management System for E.M. Cayetano Trading, a hardware
and construction supply business with two branches. The current manual
method, which relies on spreadsheets and daily cross-checks, often results in
errors and time delays. By introducing a semi-automated, web-based system, the
business will be able to streamline inventory monitoring, manage supplies more
2
effectively, and generate standardized daily, weekly, and monthly reports,
complemented by content-based report views and types, such as Summary,
Detailed, Low Stock Alert, and Category Analysis. With real-time access and
branch-wide connectivity, the proposed system is designed to improve accuracy,
efficiency, and overall business performance.
Background of the Study
E.M. Cayetano Trading is a hardware and construction supply store
established on May 25, 2012, by Ms. Edna M. Cayetano, who continues to serve
as the business admin, owner, and proprietor. The company’s main branch is
located in Manggahan, Rodriguez, Rizal, and another branch operates in San
Rafael, Rodriguez, Rizal. The business offers a wide variety of products, such as
cement, plywood, nails, hammers, screwdrivers, steel bars, and other hardware
supplies that cater to construction projects and home improvement needs.
At present, the store relies heavily on manual processes and Microsoft
Excel for tracking daily sales and inventory. Typically, one employee encodes
daily transactions while another conducts manual counts of stock. At the end of
the day, their records are compared to ensure accuracy. However, this method is
not always reliable. The admin has identified recurring issues such as inaccurate
tallies, discrepancies between actual stock and recorded inventory, and
instances of overstocking or understocking. For example, items like plywood or
cement sometimes appear to be out of stock on paper, leading to unnecessary
reorders, even when sufficient stock is still available.
3
To calculate inventory, the store follows a basic formula: Beginning
Inventory – Outgoing Sales + New Deliveries = Ending Inventory. Although
simple, this process can be error-prone, especially when data is handled
manually. Furthermore, with two branches now in operation, monitoring stock
levels across locations has become increasingly challenging. The lack of a
centralized system makes data consolidation time-consuming and inconsistent.
To address these challenges, a Web-Based Store Management System is
proposed. The system will integrate multiple branches into a single platform,
allowing the admin and authorized employees to access accurate stock
information, generate daily, weekly, and monthly reports, and receive low-stock
alerts. In addition, the reporting feature will support content-based report views
and types, including Summary, Detailed, Low Stock Alert, and Category Analysis
to provide the Admin with a clearer understanding of stock movement and
category performance across branches. The proposed system will be developed
using HTML5 and CSS3 for structure and design, paired with React v19 as the
front-end framework to enhance interactivity and responsiveness. On the server
side, JavaScript (ES2024) with Express.js version 5.1.0 will serve as the
backend technology, enabling smooth communication between the user interface
and the database. For data storage, PostgreSQL (v18) will be implemented as
the primary database because of its reliability, scalability, and ability to handle
structured records, such as inventory counts across branches. These
technologies were chosen because they are widely used in modern web
4
development, cost-efficient, and capable of ensuring both accessibility and
real-time updates for branch-wide monitoring purposes.
By adapting a web-based system, the recurring problems of inaccurate
stock monitoring, delayed reporting, and inconsistent records between branches
can be significantly reduced, if not fully eliminated. Unlike the current manual and
spreadsheet-based approaches, the proposed system ensures secured access,
real-time synchronization, and reliable storage, making inventory data accurate,
accessible, and protected from unauthorized manipulation or loss. Through this
digital transformation, E.M. Cayetano Trading will not only improve efficiency,
minimize stock errors, and maintain consistent records across its two branches
but also establish a scalable foundation that can support future business
expansion plans.

E.M. Cayetano Trading currently relies on manual and spreadsheet-based
processes to track inventory, leading to inconsistencies and inefficiencies in
managing its operations.
1. Lack of security for inventory data and monitoring, which leads to weak
access controls, vulnerability to unauthorized changes, and increased risk
of inventory mismanagement or discrepancies.
2. Absence of real-time monitoring and centralized access to inventory data
between the Manggahan and San Rafael branches.
5

centralized system for adding, updating, or maintaining user information
leads to unorganized record handling and the absence of a proper
process for authorizing or maintaining user access.

developing a Web-Based Inventory Management System that ensures accuracy,
efficiency, and accessibility.

credentials.
2. To provide a centralized platform that enables real-time monitoring of
stock and supply levels, allows product updates, and supports report
generation across both branches.
3. To design and integrate a centralized User Management feature that
enables the admin to efficiently authorize, update, and maintain user
accounts, ensuring organized access control and streamlined system
operations.

Scope
The proposed project will deliver a reliable, efficient, and user-friendly
web-based inventory management system for E.M. Cayetano Trading. Its primary
purpose is to replace the limitations of the existing manual and
spreadsheet-based processes by introducing semi-automation approach,
real-time monitoring, enhanced security, and accurate reporting across all
branches.
8
At the core of the system is the Security Module, which will manage
authentication, authorization, and password protection using bcrypt to ensure
employee credentials remain safe even in the event of a database compromise.
Complementing this is the Registration and Verification Module, which handles
account creation, prevents duplicate registrations through database-level unique
indexes, and verifies users through email.
The Maintenance Module enables the admin to perform essential upkeep
tasks, such as database backup and restore. This module also integrates User
Management functions, allowing the admin to approve new accounts, deactivate
or reactivate users, and manage user roles to maintain security and
accountability within the system.
Additionally, a Help Module will provide user assistance and guidance,
enabling employees to easily navigate the system and resolve common issues,
thereby reducing its misuse and downtime.
The system will also integrate a Search Module designed to make
inventory lookup faster and more efficient than the current system. This will
employ binary search algorithms for dealing with sorted product lists, significantly
reducing lookup time by repeatedly dividing the search space in half. For smaller
datasets or unsorted product collections, linear search will be used, as it allows
quick scanning without the overhead of sorting.
9
For reporting, the Reports Module will provide standardized daily, weekly,
and monthly reports based on the business formula “Beginning Inventory –
Outgoing Sales + New Deliveries = Ending Inventory.” It will also support
content-based report views and types (Summary, Detailed, Low Stock Alert, and
Category Analysis) to enhance data interpretation and support better
decision-making.
Central to these modules is the Inventory Module, which will record and
manage all stock movements across branches, including item codes, quantities,
prices, and categories, serving as the foundation of the system’s operations.
To further enhance business oversight, the Alerts and Notifications Module
will deliver low-stock warnings, synchronization failure alerts, and notifications for
new user registrations directly through the system. Complementing this is the
Archive Module, which securely stores inactive or outdated inventory records,
allowing an admin to restore or review them when necessary without
permanently deleting data.
For data organization, the system will apply merge sort, a
divide-and-conquer algorithm known for its stability and efficiency with large
datasets. Merge sort will ensure that product records, particularly those
containing duplicate values, are always presented in a clear and consistent order.
The development of the system will utilize JavaScript (ES2024) with the
React v19 for the front-end, Express.js version 5.1.0 for the back-end, and
PostgreSQL (v18) for the database, ensuring efficiency and reliability in data
10
management. The use of HTML5 and CSS3 will support responsive web design,
while the design of the system will be visualized using Figma. As a web-based
platform, the inventory management system will be compatible with major
operating systems such as Windows and Android, it will function through widely
used browsers including Google Chrome and Mozilla Firefox. With these features
and modules, the system is expected to optimize inventory management for E.M.
Cayetano Trading, providing a centralized solution that guarantees security,
accessibility, and accuracy in managing business resources.

An internet connection will be needed for the users to access the
inventory. The proposed system will not function without an internet connection.
Support of the system is limited to the specified operating systems, versions, and
the specified browsers stated in the scope; other OS/browsers and legacy OS
and browser versions are not guaranteed to work. Customer relationship
management, payroll, and supplier ordering modules are not included in the
11
system. Likewise, the data migration process from the existing manual or

Other limitations include the limitation of security coverage; the system
cannot guarantee absolute protection from external threats, such as hacking or

System Design
System design is the process of transforming user requirements into a
structured blueprint that defines how a system will operate and interact with its
components. According to Lal (2024), system design is both an innovative and
technical phase of development, where the proposed solution is planned and
organized in preparation for implementation. It focuses on the “how” of the
system such as how processes are structured and components interact to
achieve organizational goals. Tsui et al. (2023) emphasize that system design
bridges the gap between requirements and implementation by decomposing the
system into interconnected modules, ensuring that every function, process, and
interface works cohesively to meet both functional and non-functional
requirements.
To begin with, to visualize and communicate the design effectively, various
diagrams are used as graphical notations. These diagrams help analysts,
designers, and developers understand the system’s structure and behavior in a
more intuitive and organized manner. Each type of diagram serves a unique
purpose and represents a different perspective of the system.
Among these diagrams, the Flowchart is one of the most fundamental
tools used in system design. It graphically represents the logical flow of
processes using standardized symbols such as ovals, rectangles, and diamonds
to denote start or end points, actions, and decisions. According to Kung (2023),
flowcharts function as directed graphs that organize processing nodes, decision
13
nodes, and connecting paths to effectively model the flow of control within a
process.
Meanwhile, a Data Flow Diagram (DFD) emphasizes how data moves
between processes and entities rather than control flow. According to Borg
(2024), DFDs serve as significant modeling techniques for analyzing and
constructing information processes by illustrating the movement of information
based on inputs and outputs within a system. DFDs are important because they
model information systems clearly, allowing designers to trace data from entry to
exit points.
In addition, the Use Case Diagram, as Tsui et al. (2023) describe it as a
representation of the interactions between users (actors) and the system. It helps
identify system functionalities from the users' perspective and ensures that all
interactions and requirements are well understood during system design.
Furthermore, modern system design continues to integrate various
modeling techniques while retaining classical methods such as the Hierarchical
Input-Process-Output (HIPO) diagram. The HIPO model adopts a top-down
approach that visualizes a system’s overall structure through hierarchy charts
and Input-Process-Output (IPO) diagrams. This method effectively documents
system functions, clarifies relationships among modules, and supports clear
communication between designers and stakeholders.
Moreover, Input-Process-Output (IPO) diagrams, as described in the
INCOSE Systems Engineering Handbook (2023), represent how inputs are
14
transformed into outputs through defined system processes. Each IPO diagram
outlines what data enters a process, how it is handled, and what results it
produces. This structured visualization helps system engineers and developers
identify the essential components and expected outcomes of each module.
Lastly, the Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) focuses on the structure of
data in the system. Based on Borg (2024), an ERD "visually depicts an
organization's entities, the entities' relationships to each other, and the business
rules associated with the relationships" (p. 52). It provides a foundation for
database design by defining entities, attributes, and their interconnections.
Narrative Description of the Existing System
The existing system of E.M. Cayetano Trading operates entirely through
manual, paper-based, and spreadsheet-driven processes for managing daily
operations. The workflow mainly involves employees recording daily sales,
deliveries, and inventory updates using Microsoft Excel files. Each branch,
namely Manggahan and San Rafael, maintains its own records independently,
meaning that the admin does not consolidate or centralize their data. This
branch-by-branch setup makes it difficult to gain a unified view of stock
availability and overall business performance. The system lacks automation,
real-time tracking, and centralized storage, which often causes inconsistencies
between recorded quantities and actual stock levels.
In the current setup, each employee manually logs item transactions, such
as stock-in (deliveries) and stock-out (sales), into spreadsheets. Afterward,
15
physical stock counts are conducted at the end of the day or week to cross-check
these records. Any discrepancies require manual reconciliation, often involving
recalculating the ending inventory and verifying purchase or delivery documents.
The process is repetitive, slow, and highly prone to human error—especially
when multiple employees update the same file simultaneously.
Furthermore, since both branches operate separately, tracking inventory
across locations is difficult. The Admin must manually collect and merge files
from both locations to determine overall stock availability. This lack of
synchronization often results in overstocking of slow-moving products or
shortages of fast-moving items. Additionally, because there are no automated
low-stock alerts, restocking only happens after employees notice that supplies
are running low, which can disrupt operations or delay customer orders.
Reporting also presents significant challenges. Employees must manually
compile stock and sales data into Excel summary sheets and send them to the
admin for review. Preparing daily, weekly, or monthly inventory summaries
requires gathering multiple files, verifying formulas, and recalculating totals,
increasing the risk of misreporting. The absence of standardized reporting tools
makes it difficult for the admin to quickly assess inventory performance or detect
trends.
Moreover, the system lacks any built-in user management or data security
features. All employees share access to the same files, so accidental deletions,
unauthorized edits, or untracked changes can easily occur without accountability.
16
Overall, the existing manual system is inefficient and error-prone, limiting
the company’s ability to maintain accurate inventory records, generate timely
reports, and ensure secure data handling across branches. These limitations
highlight the need for a web-based, centralized inventory management system
that can support semi-automated stock monitoring, reporting, and user access
control—helping streamline operations and support better business decisions for
both branches.
Narrative Description of the Proposed System
The proposed system is a web-based inventory management platform
developed specifically for E.M. Cayetano Trading to replace its manual,
paper-based, and spreadsheet-driven processes. It aims to improve the
company’s inventory monitoring, reporting accuracy, and data security across its
multiple branches. By centralizing all records in a single online database, the
system enables the admin and authorized employees to access real-time,
consistent information anytime and anywhere through a secure internet
connection.
The system provides role-based access control to ensure organized and
secure user interaction. The admin holds the highest level of access, enabling
them to manage products, review reports, perform maintenance tasks such as
data backup and restoration, and oversee user accounts. Meanwhile, employees
are granted limited access that focuses on operational tasks such as updating
stock movements (stock-in and stock-out), searching items, and viewing product
details. This role-based control ensures that sensitive functions—like deactivating
17
accounts, approving new users, or editing high-level records remain exclusive to
admin.
The proposed system is composed of several interrelated modules that
enhance usability and reliability. The Security Module ensures that only
authorized users can log in through role-based authentication, secure password
handling, and account verification. The Registration and Verification Module
supports new account creation, email verification, and password reset processes,
ensuring accessibility and preventing duplicate user entries.
For system upkeep, the Maintenance Module allows an admin to perform
data backups, restore records when needed, and manage user profiles. A Help
Module is also integrated to assist users by providing FAQs, system guidelines,
and contact support, minimizing confusion and errors during use.
Central to the system is the Inventory Module, where all stock records are
stored and updated. Employees can record deliveries, sales, and adjustments
while the system automatically calculates balances. Supporting this, the Search
Module enables fast and accurate item lookups.
Supporting business oversight, the Reports Module provides
comprehensive reporting features to support business analysis and
decision-making. It generates standardized daily, weekly, and monthly reports
based on the company’s formula. In addition to time-based summaries, the
system also features content-based report views and types, such as Summary,
Detailed, Low Stock Alert, and Category Analysis, allowing the admin and
18
employees to analyze inventory trends and performance from different
perspectives. The module also includes an Export to PDF option, enabling users
to easily generate and download digital copies of reports for documentation and
review.
To further support operational accuracy, the Alerts and Notifications
Module provides real-time low-stock alerts and system-related notifications,
ensuring that the admins and employees can make timely restocking decisions
and respond promptly to inventory changes.
Complementing this, the Archive Module stores inactive or outdated
inventory items in a secure storage area within the system. This allows an admin
to manage records efficiently—viewing, filtering, or restoring archived items when
necessary while maintaining a clean and organized active inventory list.
From a technical standpoint, the system will be developed using HTML5
and CSS3 for structure and design, ensuring a clean and responsive interface.
The front end will be powered by React v19, providing dynamic and interactive
user experiences. On the server side, the system will utilize JavaScript (ES2024)
with Express.js version 5.1.0 to handle backend logic and enable seamless
communication between the client and server. For data storage, PostgreSQL
(v18) will serve as the main database, offering reliable, scalable, and structured
record management for inventory data across branches. The system will remain
accessible across devices and browsers, supporting both desktop and mobile
platforms through common browsers like Google Chrome and Mozilla Firefox.
19
Overall, the system strengthens operational efficiency by automating stock
management, securing sensitive data, and improving communication across
branches. With clear role assignments and centralized data access, E.M.
Cayetano Trading can minimize errors, generate timely reports, and make
data-driven decisions, establishing a stronger foundation for business growth and

CHAPTER 3
Methodology
This chapter discusses the methodology employed in developing the
Web-Based Store Management System, using the V-Model as the selected
software development framework. The V-Model was chosen because it
integrates validation and verification at every stage, ensuring that each phase of
development is matched with a corresponding testing phase to guarantee system
accuracy, reliability, and effectiveness. This chapter provides an overview of the
structured process followed in designing, developing, and evaluating the system.
It also discusses the data-gathering methods, such as interviews, observations,
and research, which were used to obtain accurate and relevant information for
the system’s requirements. The feasibility study is included to assess the
project’s technical, operational, and economic viability, determining whether the
system can be realistically implemented and sustained. Additionally, the chapter
presents the cost-benefit analysis to evaluate the financial soundness of the
project and a Gantt chart that visually represents the project timeline and
workflow. Altogether, these sections establish a comprehensive and systematic
methodological framework that ensures the system’s development is
well-organized, evidence-based, and aligned with its intended goals.
111
Software Process Model
Figure 86 V-Model Diagram
The diagram illustrated above is the V-Model Software Development Life
Cycle (SDLC), which is an extension of the classic waterfall model by bending
the process into a V shape. It consists of three phases: Verification, Coding, and
Validation.
It begins in the left leg, which is the Verification Phase, where the
proponents ensure that the software is developed according to the company's
requirements and technical specifications. This phase includes four design
levels: Requirements Design, System Design, Architectural Design, and Module
Design.
112
The Requirement design is where the software requirements are
understood from the customer's perspective. This phase involves thorough
communication with the company to understand their expectations and exact
requirements. The requirement gathering was done using interviews between the
company owner and proponents.
Next, in the System Design, the proponents determined the overall
structure of the web-based system, describing how its components work together
and interact. This phase will include understanding and documenting the whole
hardware and communication architecture for the product being developed.
The Architectural Design, also known as High-Level Design, is where
architectural specifications are understood and designed. The system design is
broken down further into modules taking up different functionality. The flow of
data and communication between the internal modules and other systems is
understood and defined in this stage.
The Module Design, also referred to as Low-Level Design, is where the
detailed internal design for all the system modules is defined. Each module’s
logic, input-output structure, and interconnection are defined to ensure
consistency and compatibility across the system.
The middle of the V represents the Coding or Development Phase, where
the proponents implement the gathered requirements and specifications from the
previous phase. In the V-model, the process bends upward for the final phase,
which is the validation phase. The goal of this phase is the analysis of
113
requirements; for example, the requirement design is paired with acceptance
testing, the system design is paired with system testing, and the software design
is paired with integration testing.
The Coding phase is where the actual implementation of all the design
choices from the prior phase is made. The most suitable programming language
is decided based on the system and architectural requirements, which for this
specific project is Javascript (ES2024) using VSCode (ver. 1.105) and
PostgreSQL (v18) for the system database. The coding is done according to the
coding principles and standards. Before the final build is checked into the
repository, the code is subjected to many code reviews and optimized for
maximum performance.
After the coding phase, the final phase in the V-Model is to test the
system. Unit testing is code-level testing that helps eliminate issues early on,

for invalid or missing inputs.
The Logic Tier serves as the system’s core, where all business logic,
rules, and data processing occur. Implemented using JavaScript (ES2024) and
Express.js version 5.1.0, this layer efficiently handles server-side operations,
data validation, and communication between components. It integrates bcrypt

stores all system data, including inventory records, user accounts, and generated
reports, and manages archiving, retrieval, and restoration as needed. It also
supports semi-automated data backup and recovery procedures as part of the
system’s maintenance module, ensuring business continuity in case of failures or
data corruption.
In terms of data flow, the process begins when the user interacts with the
Presentation Tier by entering input through the interface. These inputs are first
validated on the client side before being sent as requests to the Logic Tier, where
the system’s business rules and server-side processing take place. The Logic
Tier communicates with the Data Tier to store, retrieve, or update records as
required. Once the operation is completed, the processed data is sent back
through the Logic Tier to the Presentation Tier, where the results are displayed to
the user in real time. This seamless exchange of data ensures efficiency,
accuracy, and responsiveness across all system components.

Management System operates efficiently, securely, and at scale. It enables
real-time synchronization of data across branches, minimizes human error, and

provides a solid foundation for future enhancements or system expansion.

E.M. Cayetano Trading a centralized, accurate, and responsive platform for

Algorithm Implementation
The algorithm implementation of the proposed Web-Based Store
Management System for E.M. Cayetano Trading plays a crucial role in ensuring
fast, accurate, and secure operations across its major modules. Each algorithm
was carefully selected to meet the system’s functional requirements, particularly
in inventory search, product sorting, and user authentication. By integrating these
algorithms, the system minimizes manual errors, improves performance, and
enhances user experience for both the admin and employees.
The search algorithm is applied within the system’s Search Module to
enable users to quickly locate specific products from the inventory database. The
system dynamically uses two search approaches: Linear Search and Binary
135
Search. The Linear Search algorithm is used when the dataset is unsorted or
when the user performs a general keyword search. It checks each record
sequentially until a match is found, making it simple and appropriate for small
datasets or flexible keyword queries. On the other hand, the Binary Search
algorithm is implemented when the dataset is already sorted by product ID or
name. Binary Search works by repeatedly dividing the search range in half until
the target record is found, significantly reducing search time across branches
compared to a linear scan. This dual-use approach ensures that the system
remains efficient regardless of the data size or structure, providing users with
accurate and immediate results. When the dataset is unsorted or recently
updated, the system automatically defaults to Linear Search to maintain search
functionality until the data is re-sorted.
The system also integrates a sorting algorithm, specifically Merge Sort, to
organize inventory data before display and reporting. Merge Sort was chosen
due to its high stability and predictable time complexity of O(n log n), making it
ideal for handling large datasets such as hundreds of product records. The
algorithm works by recursively dividing the product list into smaller sublists,
sorting them individually, and then merging them back together in the correct
order. This ensures that all product information—such as product ID, name,
category, quantity, and last updated date—is presented in a consistent,
ascending or descending order. This not only helps the admin easily interpret the
data but also improves report generation speed and accuracy.
136
To maintain system security and data integrity, the system implements a
Bcrypt Hashing Algorithm for password encryption. When users create or log into
their accounts, the system does not store plain-text passwords. Instead, Bcrypt
transforms the entered password into an irreversible hash using a salt value.
During login, the system verifies the entered password by hashing it again and
comparing the result with the stored hash in the database. If both hashes match,
access is granted. This ensures that even if unauthorized users gain access to
the database, the actual passwords remain protected. Through this algorithm, the
system provides a strong defense against common security threats such as
brute-force and dictionary attacks.

This diagram illustrates the linear search algorithm, in which the system
starts checking the first item in the list and continues one by one until it either
finds the target item or reaches the end of the list. In the context of our system,
linear search is used for simpler lookup scenarios—such as when an employee

is found, it returns the item and ends the search; otherwise, it continues until no
more items remain. Because its performance grows proportionally with the

datasets but becomes less efficient as the inventory size grows.

branches. Because with each step the search space is halved, this method

large inventories. It requires that the list is already sorted, which aligns with our
preprocessing step in the system. This process continues until the item is found

Alvin Guillermo: Do you currently use any system or software to manage your
business operations?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: At the moment, that is what we are studying. When we
started, it was only one branch, but now we have more, and the business is
expanding. For now, I am the one who creates the systems we use.
Alvin Guillermo: Since you mentioned Excel, how do you currently manage your
business when it comes to inventory, sales records, and customer transactions?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: At the end of the day, transactions are encoded. From
those records, we generate the inventory. But not everything is tracked, only the
more saleable items that run out quickly. I also keep a manual record of daily
purchases. At the end of the day, the encoder checks if the manual and system
records match. We compute the ending inventory as: beginning balance minus
items sold, plus new deliveries.
Alvin Guillermo: What problems do you usually encounter with your manual
system?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: Sometimes the records are inaccurate. For example, the
system shows 28 pieces of plywood, but the actual count is different. That’s when
we realize something went wrong, usually after the encoder checks. It’s not yet a
smooth process.
Alvin Guillermo: How do these problems affect your daily store operations?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: Sometimes I think an item is out of stock because that’s
what the inventory shows, so I place an order. But it turns out there is still stock,
and we end up overstocked. Other times, the records show there is stock, but in
reality, there is none, so I fail to order and we run out of items to sell.
Raymond Donato: If we were to develop a system specifically for your store,
what features would you like us to add?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: Really? You will develop one? Is it free? Actually, I have a
friend who is also a programmer. He started developing one for us, but he is in
Singapore, so it was not completed. What I really need is an inventory system. In
retail stores like ours, inventory is very important, especially for saleable items
that run out quickly. I should know exactly what is in stock so I can reorder before
it runs out. That’s the most important feature.
Alvin Guillermo: What other systems would you consider helpful for your
business?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: Inventory is the priority, but payroll would also be useful,
especially now that I have more employees. If the system could also handle
communication with suppliers, that would be good. For example, having a
website where we could directly order from suppliers would be convenient.
John Alces Niez: Do you currently have suppliers? If so, how do you
communicate with them?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: Yes, we do. We usually communicate through Viber. I just
send an order, and they deliver.
John Alces Niez: Do you encounter any problems when communicating with
your suppliers?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: So far, no. But maybe it’s because younger people like you
know more modern ways. I often tell my children, who are about your age, to
study new technologies. I’m already 52, and what I know sometimes feels
outdated. You, on the other hand, are familiar with tools like AI and ChatGPT,
which make things much easier. That’s why I also ask, what new ideas can you
suggest?
John Alces Niez: One possible improvement is an inventory system. Another
suggestion is to develop a payroll feature for employees or a dedicated website
for supplier communication. For example, you could place orders through the
website, and the supplier would receive notifications for delivery.
Ms. Edna Cayetano: Actually, I don’t really have problems with my current
suppliers since I deal with them directly. It’s easier that way—I can message
them directly, even at night, and they deliver. But I also have another idea. I have
a branch in Montalban, and I thought about creating a website where customers
could place orders. Then, I could partner with other hardware stores in different
locations. For example, if someone orders from Cavite, my partner hardware
store in Cavite could fulfill the order, and I would just earn a commission. It’s still
just an idea, but it’s something I am considering.
Alvin Guillermo: That’s an interesting plan. But in terms of your current needs,
inventory is your number one priority?
Ms. Edna Cayetano: Yes, inventory is the most important. Payroll is also needed
since I now have more employees, but inventory must come first. Every store
needs an inventory management system.

Ms. Edna Cayetano: You’re welcome.
